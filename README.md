## What Are Cash Back Websites and How Do They Work?

The model is simple once you see it from the inside. When you click through a cash back site to a retailer and make a purchase, the retailer pays a commission to the cash back site for sending them a customer. The cash back site then shares some of that commission with you.

The key word there is "some." Different sites handle that split differently. Some keep a portion of the commission for themselves and pass the rest to you. Others — and this is where TopCashback stands out — pass 100% of the commission back to their members. That's the structural difference that shows up in the rates you actually see.

Here's the basic flow, step by step:

1. **Log in** to your cash back account and search for the store you want to shop at.
2. **Click the activation button** (usually labeled something like "Get Cash Back") to be redirected to the retailer's site.
3. **Shop as usual** and complete your purchase.
4. **The cash back tracks** in your account, moves from pending to confirmed, and becomes payable once the retailer's return window closes.
5. **Cash out** through your preferred method once the balance is payable.

The mechanic is the same across most platforms. Where they diverge is in how much they pay you, how many stores they cover, how flexible the payouts are, and whether they back up their rates with any kind of guarantee.

## Why Most Cash Back Sites Leave Money on the Table

The quiet catch with many cash back websites is the commission split. A site might advertise that you'll earn "up to X% cash back" at a store, but that percentage is calculated after the site has already taken its cut. You're not getting the full commission the retailer paid — you're getting a share of it.

This is the main reason rates vary so much between sites that list the same store. It's also why two people shopping the same item at the same retailer can end up with different cash back amounts depending on which portal they clicked through.
